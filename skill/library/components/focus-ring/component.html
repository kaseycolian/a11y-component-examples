Correct examples

Tab through these rather than reading them. They are the three decisions the indicator asks you to make: when it is drawn, how far out it sits, and whether it can survive the surface behind it.

1 · :focus-visible vs :focus

Click each button. The first keeps no ring — you know where you clicked, because you clicked it. The second keeps one whatever you used to get there. Now Tab through all three: every one of them rings. The text field rings on a click too, because :focus-visible already knows a caret needs a home.

2 · Offset, and the clipped ring

Tab through the four. The first reads as a heavier border rather than as a change of state. The last two sit in a box with overflow: hidden: the third’s ring is painted outside that box and clipped away, and the fourth draws its ring inside itself, so there is nothing to clip.

3 · The two-tone ring

Three backgrounds, the same two buttons on each. Tab down the left column and then the right: the accent ring is a different amount of visible on each row, and the two-tone one is not. Then change the theme in the header and do it again — the accent moves with the theme, and one of the two tones still has something to contrast with.

Common mistakes

Both are live and wrong on purpose, and neither can be found with a screen reader or an automated check. Press Tab.

4 · outline: none, live

  1. outline: none, no replacement — focus is untraceable
  2. replaced by a background tint — color alone, and forced colors take it
  3. replaced by a box-shadow ring — works, until forced colors

Tab across the three and try to say out loud where the focus is. Nothing in the markup separates them: the first is a SC 2.4.7 failure and the second a SC 1.4.1 one.

Orders — fixed

Two scrolling lists, one sticky bar each. Tab to the bottom of the left one and then Shift + Tab back up: the focused row slides under the bar. Do the same on the right and it stops below it. The difference is one scroll-margin-top on the buttons. The buttons do nothing else — they are here to take focus.
